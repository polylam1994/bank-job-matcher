How the matching works
  1. Daily exports. Bank statement lines (Xero bank feed), jobs and invoices (ServiceM8 + Xero), and job materials with supplier costs (ServiceM8).
  2. Running costs filtered out. Wages, fuel, tax, leases and subscriptions are recognised by payee and set aside.
  3. Money in: Fellegi-Sunter probabilistic linkage (Splink on DuckDB). Each payment is compared with each open invoice on amount, name, reference words and date. Every comparison adds or subtracts evidence: log2(m/u), where m is how often that agreement happens for true matches (learned from past reconciliations) and u how often it happens by chance (random sampling).
  4. One invoice, one payment. SciPy's linear_sum_assignment picks the best overall set of pairs, so two payments never claim the same invoice. A match is automatic only above 95% and well clear of the runner-up.
  5. Several payments, one invoice. Part payments from the same payer are combined by subset-sum to find instalments that add up to what is still owed.
  6. Money out: one payment, many material lines. Statement suppliers (Reece, Tradelink) are matched to a contiguous statement period of lines; card purchases (Bunnings) to same-day lines that add up exactly. A wider subset-sum search is the last resort and always goes to a person, because coincidental exact sums are common.
